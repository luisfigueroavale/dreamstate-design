## First-draft (v1) facts
- v1 palette (from the brand site): blue gradient `#071A4F` to `#1450D9` to `#4A86F7`, white cards, Inter. v1 canvas 1080x1350, 8.3 s loop (2.3 s build + 6 s pulses), 15 fps GIF at 720x900.
- v1 structure: kicker, 2-line title, 8 to 12 rounded nodes joined by wires with travelling pulses, one 3-step playbook strip, one CTA line. It was judged too simple: one hero-less diagram, one kind of motion.
- Owner direction behind the posts: complex graphics with many moving parts, a playbook at the bottom, hook plus graphic builds the brand; inspiration from Dima Bilous, Michel Lieben and Kallaway-style visuals; posts should split readers without being toxic.

## Post 1: The GTM team you can clone (giveaway, tag: signup)
Graphic: `gtm-team-pipeline.gif`

I'm convinced you can replace half of a GTM team with a GitHub repo.

The people who close deals stay. The copy-paste work goes.

Look at what a normal outbound week is:
Someone builds a list.
Someone writes the first email.
Someone loads the sequence.
Someone drafts comments.
Someone answers replies.
Someone plans content.
Someone chases the deal that went quiet.

Seven jobs. Seven tools. Seven Slack threads asking "is this live yet?"

And every one of those seats is waiting on someone else's spreadsheet.

So we open sourced the alternative.

The Dreamstate GTM team is 16 Grok Bots, each with one lane:
→ Lead Sourcer builds and scores the list
→ Outbound Copywriter writes first touch email and LinkedIn notes
→ Sequences & Deliverability loads it and watches mailbox health
→ Social Engagement drafts comments on the right posts
→ Reply Manager drafts replies in live threads
→ Head of Content plans the week for the LinkedIn, X and YouTube writers
→ Chief of Staff hands file work to Claude Code or Codex

One rule runs all of it: nothing sends, posts or spends until you say yes to that exact item.

You stay the approver. The repo does the busywork.

It's free. Comment TEAM and I'll send you the repo and the setup prompt.

## Post 3: Signals in 2026 (personal, tag: reach)
Graphic: `signals-brain.gif`

Everyone thinks buying signals look like this:
One website visit. One form fill. Book the meeting.

Then they look at reality:
20 tools. 20 dashboards. 20 logins. A Zapier chain held together with hope.

Champion tracking in one tab. Funding rounds in another. Ad engagement in a third. Post engagement somewhere nobody checks.

By the time someone connects the dots, the buyer already took a call with your competitor.

Here's what the real world needs: one brain.

That's what we built Dreamstate to be. We own the whole stack ourselves, from the data to the outreach to the posting. No duct-taped vendors in the middle.

It's also why it costs us about $7 a month to run a customer while others spend around $50.

Collecting signals is easy. Acting on all of them from one place wins the deal.
